Research rewards a particular kind of patience: you set the conditions carefully, then wait to find out
what the material actually did. I seem to want the same thing outside work — long walks toward a ridge
with no certainty about the view, and long stories that take their time getting where they are going.

## Hiking, camping, and travelling

Most of my walking has been in hill country: bamboo groves, river crossings, and trails that turn to
mud in the monsoon. What keeps me going back is less the summit than the rhythm of it — carrying what
you need, covering ground slowly, and ending the day somewhere you could not have driven to.

## Novels, films, and television

I read novels constantly, and watch films and series with the same appetite. It is the closest thing I
have to the opposite of engineering: nothing to optimise, no parameter to tune, no result to validate.
Just someone else's world, built carefully, that you agree to live inside for a while.

A good story and a good experiment do share one quality, though. Both are ruined by knowing the ending
too early, and both are worth staying with when they refuse to explain themselves immediately.
