## Graduate Student Researcher

**The University of Texas at Arlington** — Arlington, Texas, USA
_August 2021 – Present_

Experimental and computational study of interlayer bonding, void formation, and fracture behavior in extrusion-based additive manufacturing.

- Integrated MWIR thermography, X-ray μ-CT, tensile/DMA/Mode-I testing, and SEM fractography to experimentally anchor the linkage among interfacial thermal history, void morphology, and structural response.
- Designed and analyzed a randomized 2×3×5 full-factorial program across 60 print batches using GLM/ANOVA, Type III sums of squares, interaction and residual diagnostics, and Tukey HSD.
- Quantified in-situ annealing gains in ABS-CF: **39% higher ultimate tensile strength**, 30% higher elastic modulus, 63% higher glassy storage modulus, and roughly **92% higher Mode-I fracture resistance**, with crack-path voids reduced from about 4.0% to 0.7%.
- Developed μ-CT-informed Abaqus RVEs for standard and in-situ-annealed bead architectures, with three-axis virtual homogenization, mesh convergence, and low/mid/high engineering-property envelopes.
- Constructed Abaqus/Explicit DCB and SENB cohesive-zone models, and performed ASTM D5528 and ASTM D5045 testing to calibrate and validate cohesive laws.
- Designed and prototyped adaptive in-situ annealing printheads using GD&T and DFM/DFA, resulting in **one issued and one pending U.S. patent**.
- Created supervised machine-learning models to predict mechanical performance from process parameters and thermal features, reducing trial-and-error in parameter selection.
